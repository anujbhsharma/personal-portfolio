The problem

Every solo developer has pushed a .env file, a hardcoded token, or an API key to GitHub at least once — and bots scan public commits for secrets within minutes. One leaked key can mean a drained account, a hijacked service, or a very expensive surprise bill. Most developers know they should scan their repos; almost nobody has a routine for it.

How this helps

LEAKLOCK gives you that routine: a professional scanning playbook that walks through what to look for, where secrets hide (history included, not just the current tree), and how to remediate when you find one — plus tooling to automate the checks so they keep running after you've read the playbook once.

What’s inside

  • A pro secret-scanning playbook: what to scan, in what order, and how often
  • Coverage of current files and git history, where old keys linger
  • Tooling to automate scans on your repos
  • Remediation steps: revoking, rotating, and cleaning up after a leak

Who it’s for

  • Solo developers and indie hackers who push to GitHub
  • Anyone who's ever hardcoded a key and wants to make sure it's not still out there
  • Builders who want a repeatable security routine without enterprise tooling

What it’s not

  • This doesn't undo a leak that already happened — if a key is exposed, rotate it immediately regardless.
  • It's a playbook and tooling, not a guarantee; no scanner catches everything.
  • It won't monitor for you forever — you still need to run the routine.
